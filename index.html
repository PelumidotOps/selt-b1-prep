Speaking Simulator

Simulate real 1-on-1 interlocutor interview (~12 mins)

Target: 50%
Part 1: Personal Info & Spelling ~3 mins

The examiner will ask your name, ask you to spell your surname, and ask 2–3 questions about your routine or work.

Spelling Trap: Candidates often hesitate on English letters! Practice: A, E, I, O, U, G, J, Y, W.
Part 2: Situational Role-Play ~2 mins

The examiner sets up a real-world everyday situation. You initiate or respond naturally.

Formula: 1. Greet politely → 2. State the issue → 3. Propose a solution or ask for help.
Part 3: Shared Decision Making ~3 mins

You and the examiner look at a set of options (e.g. activities for care residents) and discuss what to choose together.

Golden Rule: Ask the examiner's opinion! Don't talk alone. Use: "What do you think about...?", "Shall we also consider...?"
Part 4: 1.5-Min Topic Talk ~4 mins

You get a topic card + 30 seconds to make notes + you must speak for 1.5 minutes uninterrupted.

The P.R.E.E.P Strategy:
• Point: Introduce your topic.
• Reason: Explain why it's important.
• Example: Give a personal story or detail.
• Effect / Feeling: How it affected you.
• Point Summary: Wrap up with 1 sentence.

Writing Studio

Total: 70 mins
Task 1 Rules (Informal / Semi-Formal) 70 – 100 words

Usually an email to a friend or colleague. You must address all 3 bullet points given in the prompt!

The 3-Paragraph Formula:
• Greeting + Purpose: "Hi John, I hope you are well. I am writing to..."
• Body (Bullets 1 & 2): Give details using because, also, however.
• Closing + Sign-off: "Let me know what you think. Best regards, [Name]"
Task 2 Rules (Formal Letter / Article) 100 – 120 words

A formal letter or short article expressing an opinion, discussing pros/cons, or suggesting improvements.

The Universal 4-Paragraph Structure:
• P1: Intro (20w): "In recent years, [Topic] has become very important. In this article, I will share my opinion."
• P2: Argument 1 (35w): "Firstly, ... For example, ... As a result, ..."
• P3: Argument 2 (35w): "Furthermore, ... In addition, ..."
• P4: Conclusion (20w): "To conclude, I strongly believe that..."

Listening & Reading Hacks

Avoid common trap answers & pass in 50%+

3 Rules to Beat the Listening Traps

  • The Distractor Trap: The speaker will mention Option A, but then say: "Oh wait, actually...", "On second thought...". Don't pick the first word you hear!
  • Audio Plays TWICE: First listen = get the general idea. Second listen = confirm your exact answer and check spelling.
  • Pre-Read Questions: In the 30 seconds before audio starts, underline keywords like dates, times, and places.

B1 Lifesaver Phrases

Never freeze in front of the examiner

1. Phrases to Buy Time (Instead of Staying Silent!)

If you freeze, examiners deduct marks for silence. Use these while you think:

"That is an interesting question, let me think..."
"Well, from my perspective..."
"To be completely honest with you..."
2. If You Didn't Understand the Question

Asking the examiner to repeat is 100% allowed and does NOT fail you!

"Could you please repeat that for me, please?"
"Could you rephrase that, please?"
"Sorry, did you mean...?"
