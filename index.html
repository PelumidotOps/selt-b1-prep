1 Speaking Simulator

1-on-1 interlocutor interview (~12 minutes)

4 Parts
Part 1 • Personal & Spelling ~3 mins

The examiner will ask your name, ask you to spell your surname letter-by-letter, and ask 2–3 questions about your routine or care work.

⚠️
Spelling Trap: Candidates lose easy marks by confusing letters. Practice pronunciation of vowels (A, E, I, O, U) and tricky letters (G vs J, Y, W).
Part 2 • Situational Roleplays ~2 mins

The examiner acts as your care supervisor or colleague. You must start or respond to everyday situations with polite English.

The 3-Step Formula:
1. Polite Greeting ("Hello, could I speak with you?")
2. State Problem clearly ("I have an urgent family matter...")
3. Offer Solution ("Maria agreed to cover my shift...")
Part 3 • Shared Discussion ~3 mins

You and the interlocutor will look at options and decide on something together (e.g., care home activity, healthy menu).

Top Tip: Don't give a monologue! Always ask the examiner: "What is your opinion on that?", "Do you agree that music is better?"
Part 4 • 90-Second Speech ~4 mins

You have 30 seconds to prepare notes on a card, then speak for 75 to 90 seconds uninterrupted.

The P.R.E.E.P Strategy:
Point (Topic) → Reason (Why) → Example (Story) → Effect (Impact) → Point wrap-up.

2 Writing Studio

Master Task 1 & Task 2 (Total 70 mins)

50% Pass
Task 1 Rules • Informal Email 70 – 100 words

Usually an email to a friend or supervisor. You must answer all 3 bullet points in the question to get maximum task marks!

The 3-Paragraph Formula:
• Paragraph 1 (Greeting & Purpose): "Hi [Name], I hope you are well. I am writing to tell you..."
• Paragraph 2 (Main details): Address points using because, also, however.
• Paragraph 3 (Sign off): "Let me know what you think. Best wishes, [Name]"
Task 2 Rules • Formal Article/Letter 100 – 120 words

A short formal composition or letter expressing your opinion on a health or community topic.

Universal 4-Paragraph Skeleton:
• P1 Intro: "In recent years, [Topic] has become extremely important..."
• P2 Argument 1: "Firstly, ... For example, ... As a result, ..."
• P3 Argument 2: "Furthermore, ... In addition, ..."
• P4 Conclusion: "To conclude, I strongly believe that..."

3 Listening & Reading Hacks

Avoid the trap answers & secure 50%+

🛡️ 3 Rules to Beat the Listening Traps

  • The Distractor Trap: The speaker says Option A first, then pauses and says: "Actually, on second thought...", "The supervisor changed it to...". Never pick the first word you hear!
  • Audio Plays TWICE: 1st listen: get the main answer. 2nd listen: verify spelling and exact details.
  • Pre-Read Questions: In the 30s pause before audio starts, underline dates, times, and names.

4 Lifesaver Phrases

Never freeze in front of the examiner

1. Phrases to Buy Time (Avoid Silence!)

Examiners deduct marks for silence. Use these phrases while your brain thinks:

"That's an interesting question, let me think..."
"Well, from my perspective..."
"To be completely honest with you..."
2. Didn't Understand the Question?

Asking the examiner to repeat is 100% permitted and is standard B1 communication!

"Could you please repeat that for me?"
"Could you rephrase that, please?"
