1 Speaking Simulator

Real interlocutor interview practice (~12 mins)

4 Parts
Part 1 • Personal Info & Surname Spelling ~3 mins

The examiner will ask your name, ask you to spell your surname out loud, and ask 2–3 questions about your routine or care work.

⚠️
Spelling Trap: Candidates lose easy marks by confusing letters. Practice pronunciation of vowels (A, E, I, O, U) and tricky letters (G vs J, Y, W).
Part 2 • Situational Roleplays ~2 mins

The examiner acts as your care supervisor or colleague. You must start or respond politely.

The 3-Step Formula:
1. Polite Greeting ("Hello, could I speak with you?")
2. State Problem ("I have an urgent appointment...")
3. Offer Solution ("Maria agreed to cover my shift...")
Part 3 • Shared Discussion ~3 mins

Discuss options together with the interlocutor (e.g. care resident activities).

Top Tip: Don't give a monologue! Always ask the examiner: "What is your opinion on that?", "Do you agree that music is better?"
Part 4 • 90-Second Speech ~4 mins

You get 30 seconds to make notes, then speak for 75 to 90 seconds uninterrupted.

The P.R.E.E.P Strategy:
Point (Topic) → Reason (Why) → Example (Story) → Effect (Impact) → Point wrap-up.

2 Writing Studio

Master Task 1 & Task 2 (Total 70 mins)

50% Pass
Task 1 Rules • Informal Email 70 – 100 words

Usually an email to a friend or supervisor. Address all 3 bullet points in the question to get maximum marks!

The 3-Paragraph Formula:
• P1 (Greeting & Purpose): "Hi [Name], I hope you are well. I am writing to tell you..."
• P2 (Main Details): Address points using because, also, however.
• P3 (Sign off): "Let me know what you think. Best wishes, [Name]"
Task 2 Rules • Formal Article/Letter 100 – 120 words

Formal composition expressing your opinion on a health or community topic.

Universal 4-Paragraph Skeleton:
• P1 Intro: "In recent years, [Topic] has become extremely important..."
• P2 Argument 1: "Firstly, ... For example, ... As a result, ..."
• P3 Argument 2: "Furthermore, ... In addition, ..."
• P4 Conclusion: "To conclude, I strongly believe that..."

3 Listening & Reading Hacks

Beat the trap answers & pass in 50%+

🛡️ 3 Rules to Beat Listening Traps

  • The Distractor Trap: The speaker says Option A first, then pauses: "Actually, on second thought...", "The supervisor changed it to...". Never pick the first word you hear!
  • Audio Plays TWICE: 1st listen: general answer. 2nd listen: verify spelling and exact time.
  • Pre-Read Questions: In the 30s pause, underline dates and keywords.

4 Lifesaver Phrases

Never freeze in front of the examiner

1. Phrases to Buy Time (Avoid Silence!)

Silence loses marks. Use these natural phrases to give your brain 5 seconds to think:

"That's an interesting question, let me think..."
"Well, from my perspective..."
"To be completely honest with you..."
2. Didn't Understand the Question?

Asking the examiner to repeat is 100% permitted and gets normal marks:

"Could you please repeat that for me?"
"Could you rephrase that, please?"
